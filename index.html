Do we breathe the same air?

Same outdoor air, different air indoors and on the move. We measure campus air with fixed and wearable sensors, model personal exposure and share it openly in one app.

What we built

One open platform, from sensor to phone

  • Outdoor sensor: rooftop PurpleAir PA-II, every 2 min, US EPA-corrected.
  • Indoor sensor: PurpleAir in a lecture hall (Chemical and Environmental Engineering building).
  • Wearable sensors (Atmotube): 5 devices, field sessions across campus.
  • One open app: corrected data, Türkiye national AQI, health advice, alerts when campus AQI exceeds 100, a daily summary at 08:00 and a personal exposure assistant.

Results

What the data show

Measured · average day, outdoor sensor

12.7 µg/m³

Period mean PM2.5, May–Oct 2026 (not an annual mean). Peak at 06:00 (16.5 µg/m³), lowest at 18:00 (9.4 µg/m³). 42 of 154 complete days were above the WHO 24-hour guideline level (15 µg/m³); WHO annual guideline: 5 µg/m³.

Rooftop PurpleAir PA-II, US EPA-corrected · 3,751 valid hours · 154 complete days (≥ 18 h) · 1 May – 12 Oct 2026.

Modelled · same walk, different hour

−38% PM2.5 inhaled

One 1-hour brisk walk on a typical weekday: 22.9 µg inhaled at 06:00–09:00 versus 14.1 µg at 16:00–19:00.

Median of 107 weekdays (14.7 and 9.0 µg/m³) × 1.56 m³/h (US EPA 2011); outdoor sensor, May–Oct 2026.

Modelled · illustrative scenario, not a measured person

2.3× the dose

Two hours in the wrong places (36.5 µg) versus eight hours in the right places (16.2 µg).

Hourly PM2.5 (May–Oct 2026) × building factor (wearables, 27–30 Apr) × indoor factor 0.5 × breathing rate.

In development

Forecasting personal exposure with machine learning: training data from sensors, wearable sessions and weather; explainable hour-by-hour forecasts; source characterization; planned forecast-based early warning.

Burning fossil fuels drives both climate change and much of the air pollution we breathe: from campus to city, open low-cost tools can link clean air and climate action.
